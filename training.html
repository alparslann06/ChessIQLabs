The roadmap

Five habits that move rating.

1

Review every serious game

No coaching replaces your own review. Run the analyzer, read the report, find the one move that decided the game — that's your curriculum.

TOOL → GAME ANALYSIS · 10 MIN/GAME
2

Woodpecker repetitions

Solve the same set of tactics again and again until the patterns are reflexes. Speed, then accuracy, then speed again — the method behind most professional rating jumps.

TOOL → PUZZLES · SETS OF 20 · 3 PASSES
3

Blunder-proofing checklist

Before every move: checks, captures, threats. It sounds trivial; done on every move for a month, it eliminates the majority of sub-1600 losses on its own.

HABIT → DURING GAMES · ZERO TIME COST
4

Openings by understanding, not memorization

Learn the plans and pawn structures of your repertoire. Ten deep lines beat fifty shallow ones — and survive when the opponent leaves theory on move five.

TOOL → OPENING DRILLS · SOON
5

Endgames before openings

Basic rook endings and king activity decide more rating points below 2000 than any opening novelty. Drill the fundamentals until they're boring.

TOOL → ENDGAME TRAINER · SOON

Pattern library

Know your leaks by name.

The report classifies every mistake into a recurring pattern. Most players have two or three that explain nearly all their losses.

MOST COMMON

Hanging pieces

Undefended pieces left on attacked squares. Fixed by the pre-move checklist, not talent.

TACTICAL

Back-rank blindness

Ignoring the last rank. One glance at f8/h8 before committing is worth 50 Elo.

STRATEGIC

Premature attacks

Attacking before development is complete. If two pieces are asleep, the attack is fiction.
